A **sample** is a physical batch made from a [variant](/docs/research/variants). Where a
variant is the formula on paper, a sample is a jar actually mixed on the bench, then
prepared, labeled, and measured. A variant can have many samples over the life of an
experiment, for example a first attempt and a corrected re-make.

Samples live on the **Samples** tab of an experiment.

![Experiment samples](/modules/research/samples.png)

## Creating a sample

A sample is created from a specific variant and captures:

- **Label**: a short name for the sample.
- **Size**: the amount made, with a **unit of measurement** (grams, ounces, and so on).
- **Variant**: the formulation the sample is made from.

Each sample is assigned a unique reference code, formatted `S-01`. This code is what the
QR label and the scan workflow use to identify the sample.

## QR labels and scanning

A sample can be printed as a **QR label** on a label printer straight from its
card. The label carries the sample's reference and a QR code that encodes the sample. Check out [printers in the hardware section](/docs/hardware/printers).

Scanning that QR code anywhere Lumexia is listening for scans opens the sample's
experiment and deep-links directly to the sample on the Samples tab. In practice you can
print a label, stick it on the jar, and later scan the jar to pull up its formulation,
preparation state, and measurements without searching for it.

## Preparing a sample

![Preparation](/modules/research/sample-preparation.png)

Making a sample is tracked as a **preparation** checklist derived from its variant's
formulation. Each material in the variant becomes a preparation step, and you toggle each
one as it is added to the batch. This gives an ingredient-by-ingredient record of what
actually went in.

When every material has been added, the sample is marked **prepared**, which stamps who
prepared it and when. A sample can also be marked back to **unprepared** if you need to
correct the record.

<Callout>
  The preparation checklist comes from the variant's materials at the time you work
  through it. It records that each ingredient was added; it is a completion log, not a
  scale reading. Quantities to weigh come from the variant's concentrations applied to the
  sample size.
</Callout>

## Recording measurements

![measurements](/modules/research/measuring.png)

Once a sample exists you can record **QC measurements** against it. A measurement ties the
sample to a [QC parameter](/docs/quality/parameters) from the quality module, such as pH,
viscosity, or appearance, and stores the measured **value**.

Two details make measurements flexible:

- **Runs**: the same parameter can be measured more than once on a sample. Each reading is
  a numbered **run**, so you can record a value at 24 hours, at 48 hours, and so on, and
  keep them all.
- **Structured inputs**: parameters that define multiple input fields (through their
  [input definitions](/docs/quality/parameters)) capture each field on the measurement,
  not just a single number.

Because R&D uses the same parameter definitions as production quality control, a reading
taken on the bench is described in exactly the same terms as one taken on a released
batch.

## Sample notes and files

Like the experiment itself, each sample has its own **notes** and **files** panels. Sample
notes use the same shared, color-coded [note types](/docs/research/experiments#notes-and-files)
as experiment notes, and both feed into the experiment's aggregated Notes and Files tabs.

## Copy for LLM

Each sample has a **Copy for LLM** button that exports its formulation, the sample's
details, its QC measurements, and its notes as markdown for pasting into an AI assistant.
See [Copy for LLM](/docs/research/experiments#copy-for-llm).
