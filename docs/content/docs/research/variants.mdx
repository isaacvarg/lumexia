A **variant** is a single candidate formulation inside an experiment. It has two parts: a
**formulation** (the materials and how much of each, by concentration) and a **method**
(the ordered steps for making it). An experiment usually holds several variants so that
different formulas can be built, sampled, and compared against one another.

Variants live on the **Variants** tab of an experiment.

![Experiment variants](/modules/research/variants.png)

## The formulation

Each variant carries a table of **materials**. Every line is an
[inventory item](/docs/inventory/items) plus a **concentration** expressed as **% w/w**
(percent by weight of the total formula). This mirrors how an
[MBPR](/docs/production/mbpr) stores its bill of materials, so a variant reads like a
recipe rather than a fixed batch: it describes proportions, not pounds.

Materials can be added, edited, removed, and dragged to reorder. A running **total**
concentration is shown so you can see at a glance whether the formula sums to 100%.

<Callout>
  Concentrations are stored internally as fractions between 0 and 1 but are always
  displayed and entered as percentages. A material at `0.05` internally shows as **5%**.
</Callout>

### Phases

Materials can be grouped into **phases**, a free-text label such as "Water phase",
"Oil phase", or "Cool down". Phases organize a formulation the way a chemist thinks about
it, and they group the material rows visually. You can:

- Assign a material to a phase, or move it to a different one.
- Rename a phase, which updates every material in it.
- Reorder materials within and across phases.

A material with no phase simply sits ungrouped. Phases are per variant; they are not a
shared catalog.

## The method

Below the formulation, a variant carries an ordered **method**: the numbered steps for
actually making the formula. Each step has a **sequence**, an optional **phase** label,
and free-text **content**. Steps can be added, edited, deleted, and dragged to reorder.
Where the formulation says what goes in, the method says how it is combined.

## Creating an analog from an MBPR

A variant can be built from scratch, or cloned from an existing
[MBPR](/docs/production/mbpr) with **Add Analog**. This is the fastest way to start from a
recipe the facility already produces and then tweak it.

Choosing an MBPR and a label creates a variant that copies:

- **Materials**: every line of the MBPR's bill of materials, converted from the MBPR's
  percentage into the variant's fractional concentration, ordered by step sequence, and
  tagged with each material's step **phase**.
- **Method**: the MBPR's active step instructions, flattened into an ordered list of
  method steps in step-sequence order, each tagged with its step's phase. Archived steps
  and archived instructions are skipped.

The new variant records which MBPR it came from (its **source MBPR**). That link is what
powers the reverse view: an MBPR's **R&D** tab lists every experiment that has a variant
sourced from it, so you can see all the experimentation happening around a given recipe.

<Callout>
  An analog is a one-time copy, not a live link. Editing the variant afterward does not
  change the MBPR, and later changes to the MBPR do not flow back into the variant. The
  source-MBPR reference is kept only so the experiment shows up on that MBPR's R&D tab.
</Callout>

## Copy for LLM

Each variant has a **Copy for LLM** button that exports just that formulation, its method,
and its samples and measurements as markdown for pasting into an AI assistant. See
[Copy for LLM](/docs/research/experiments#copy-for-llm).
