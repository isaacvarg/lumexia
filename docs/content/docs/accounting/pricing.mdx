![Examination Dashboard](/modules/accounting/examination-dashboard.png)

A **pricing examination** is an audit of what an item costs and what its finished
products should sell for. It gathers the item's current cost structure,
recalculates the consumer price of each finished product so that it clears a
healthy profit margin, then archives a full snapshot of those numbers. The
result is a reviewed, approved price backed by a record of exactly how it was
derived.

## Why pricing examinations exist

Costs move. A material's purchase price rises, arrival costs change, a
formulation gets harder to make, and suddenly a product that used to be
profitable is quietly selling at a loss. This is the problem Lumexia was
[born to solve](/docs#motivations): during a period of rapid scale-up, material
availability and pricing swung wildly, and the ripple effects on finished-product
pricing were nearly impossible to keep straight on paper and in spreadsheets.

A pricing examination makes re-pricing deliberate and traceable. Instead of
guessing, someone examines the real cost inputs, dials the numbers until every
container clears the margin target, and sends the result for review. Every
examination is archived, so you can always see what a price was, when it changed,
and why.

## What triggers an examination

An examination can start three ways:

- **Automatically on reception.** When an item is received, if its item type has
  the pricing-examination trigger enabled (set in **Settings > Inventory > Item
  Types**), Lumexia enqueues an examination for that item. This is how a fresh
  purchase price automatically prompts a pricing review.
- **Manually.** From the pricing landing page, use **Begin Pricing Examination**,
  search for an item, and start one by hand.
- **After a rejection.** Rejecting an examination automatically queues a new one
  for the same item (see [Rejection](#rejection)).

## The examination lifecycle

An examination moves through a small set of statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Queued** | Created and waiting to be conducted. |
| **Pending Review** | Conducted and completed; awaiting a reviewer's decision. |
| **Approved** | Accepted; the recomputed prices stand. |
| **Rejected** | Sent back; a fresh Queued examination is created in its place. |

## The pricing landing page

The landing page at `/accounting/pricing` opens on two tabs.

- **Overview** shows two cards side by side: the **Queue** (examinations waiting
  to be conducted) and **Latest** (the most recent examinations, with their
  examined item, who conducted them, when, and their status).
- **All Examinations** lists every examination.

Clicking a queued examination opens the conduct screen. **Configure Templates**
sits at the top right; see [Templates](#templates).

## Conducting an examination

![Examination](/modules/accounting/examination.png)

How an examination is conducted depends on how the item is sourced:

- **Purchased items** are priced from their pricing data and their most recent
  purchase price, converted to a cost per pound.
- **Produced items** are priced from their active [MBPR](/docs/production/mbpr)'s
  bill of materials, rolled up into a cost per batch and per pound.

In both cases the building block is the item's cost. An item's cost is its price
per unit plus its arrival cost plus an allowance for unforeseen difficulties.
The price per unit is the item's upcoming price when one is set and active;
otherwise it is the last purchase price.

### Modifying the numbers to ensure a proper profit

For each finished product you set a price, and the point of the examination is to
make sure that price leaves enough margin. The **Alter By** control lets you
drive the pricing from whichever figure you think in: **Consumer Price**,
**Markup %**, **Profit**, or **Profit Margin**. Enter a value for one and the
other three are back-solved automatically, so you can nudge any single number and
immediately see the rest.

The four figures relate to the finished product's total cost like this:

| Figure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Consumer Price** | cost × (1 + markup / 100) |
| **Markup %** | (consumer price − cost) ÷ cost × 100 |
| **Profit** | consumer price − cost |
| **Profit Margin** | (consumer price − cost) ÷ consumer price × 100 |

A finished product is only considered valid once its profit margin clears the
target. Products color red while they fall short and green once they pass, so you
can see at a glance which containers still need attention.

<Callout>
  The target profit margin is currently fixed at **15%** in code rather than
  being configurable. A finished product is flagged valid only when its profit
  margin exceeds 15%.
</Callout>

## Templates

Building out every finished-product container by hand for each examination is
tedious, especially when the same set of container sizes recurs across many
items. A **pricing template** is a reusable blueprint of finished products: each
one carries its name, fill quantity, declared quantity, free-shipping cost, fill
unit of measurement, a difficulty adjustment, and any auxiliaries (extra items
like caps or pumps). A template can be scoped to a specific item type or left
generic so it applies to any item.

Templates are managed at `/accounting/pricing/templates` through a wizard,
reachable from **Configure Templates** on the landing page.

While conducting an examination, **Apply Template** instantiates a template's
containers onto the item, pre-filled with their cost structure but with the
pricing figures left at zero. You then only have to dial in the margin for each
container instead of rebuilding it from scratch.

## Completing and review

When the numbers are set, **Complete** archives a snapshot of everything: the
item's (or produced item's) pricing data and each finished product, including its
consumer price, markup, profit, and profit margin. The examination then moves to
**Pending Review**.

The details screen (`/accounting/pricing/details`) is where a reviewer looks it
over. It shows:

- **Basics**: the item, its status, the cost breakdown (overall item cost,
  arrival cost, production usage cost, auxiliary usage cost, unforeseen
  difficulties cost), and, once decided, who approved or rejected it and when.
- **Notes**: reviewer notes, each with a type and optional file attachments.
- Tabs for **Finished Products** (each container's archived pricing) and
  **Pricing Parameters** (the bill-of-materials or container parameters behind
  the numbers).

The **Approve** and **Reject** buttons appear only while the examination is
Pending Review. Approving records the approver and the time; the recomputed
prices stand.

## Rejection

Rejecting an examination does more than mark it declined. It sets the examination
to **Rejected**, records who rejected it and when, then creates a fresh
**Queued** examination for the same item linked back to the one it replaced.
Crucially, the reviewer's notes are copied forward onto the new examination, so
whoever picks it up next sees the feedback that prompted the redo. Rejection is
therefore both an audit trail and an automatic re-queue.

## Viewing an item's past examinations

Every item has a pricing overview reachable from its item details. It shows a
**Last Examined** panel, trend charts of how the item's cost, its bill-of-material
cost, and its finished-product prices have moved across examinations over time,
and a table of the full examination history. Clicking any row in that table opens
that past examination's detail screen, so you can review exactly what was decided
and why at any point in the item's history.
