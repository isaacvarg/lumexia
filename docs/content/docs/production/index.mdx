The production module is where an item is actually manufactured. It turns a
recipe into a repeatable, traceable batch: what goes in, in what order, who did
each part, who verified it, and what came out. Everything is anchored to two
records that sound alike but do very different jobs: the **MBPR** and the
**BPR**.

## MBPR vs BPR

- A **Master Batch Production Record (MBPR)** is the reusable **template** for
  producing one item. It holds the bill of materials (as concentrations), the
  ordered steps and instructions, and the batch sizes the item can be made in. An
  MBPR is written once and reused for every batch.
- A **Batch Production Record (BPR)** is a single **run** made from an MBPR at a
  chosen batch size. Creating a BPR copies the MBPR's recipe into concrete
  quantities (in pounds) and per-run tasks, then that batch is scheduled, staged,
  compounded, verified, and completed.

So the MBPR is the recipe; the BPR is one time you cook it. One MBPR produces
many BPRs over its life.

## The four areas

Production is organized into four areas, each with its own page:

1. **MBPR**: authoring and versioning the master recipe: steps, materials,
   instructions, batch sizes, and vessels.
2. **Planning**: requesting a batch (creating a BPR), checking material
   sufficiency, allocating stock, and scheduling.
3. **Compounding**: the shop-floor work: staging materials and executing the
   batch steps.
4. **Production Quality**: independent primary and secondary verification of
   staged materials and flagged steps, which gates the batch's progress.

## The batch lifecycle

A batch moves through a defined set of statuses. Most transitions happen
automatically as work is completed; some can be set by hand. In broad strokes:

1. A batch is **requested** in Planning, creating a **Draft** BPR from an MBPR.
2. Planning confirms material sufficiency and schedules the batch, which becomes
   **Queued**.
3. On the floor, materials are **staged** and verified (**Staging Materials**).
4. Once every material is verified, the batch moves to **Compounding** and the
   steps are worked.
5. When all steps are done, the batch is **Completed**, its materials are
   consumed from inventory, and it moves to **Awaiting QC** for
   [quality](/docs/quality) testing, then **Released**.

The full status set:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | Requested or anticipated; not yet being allocated. |
| **Allocating Materials** | Material availability is being checked and stock allocated to the batch. |
| **Verifying BOM Fulfillment** | Whether materials can fulfill the batch is in question; an inventory audit is likely. |
| **Awaiting Materials** | A material's stock is blocking the batch. |
| **Known Material Arrival** | Blocked by a material, but that material has a known arrival date. |
| **Queued** | On the batch schedule, ready to stage. |
| **Staging Materials** | Materials are being staged and verified. |
| **Compounding** | Actively being made. |
| **Completed** | Physically finished; steps all complete. |
| **Awaiting QC** | Waiting for micro and in-house quality control. |
| **Released** | Released for use/sale. |
| **Investigating / Corrective Actions** | Something went wrong and is being investigated. |
| **Failed** | Corrective actions could not save the batch; scrapped. |
| **Consumption Error** | An error occurred consuming the batch's materials and must be resolved. |

<Callout>
  Statuses can be advanced manually from a BPR's Status card ("Change Status
  To…"), but the everyday flow (Queued → Staging → Compounding → Completed →
  Awaiting QC) is driven automatically by Lumexia as each stage's work finishes.
  See [Planning](/docs/production/planning) for the details.
</Callout>
