title: Inventory Quantities
description: On hand, on order, allocated, available — what each quantity means and how it's calculated

Lumexia describes an item's stock with several distinct quantities. They sound
similar but mean different things, and confusing them is the most common source
of inventory misunderstanding. This page defines each one precisely.

<Callout>
  **On-hand stock is not a stored number.** Lumexia never keeps a single
  "quantity" field that gets edited up and down. Instead, every
  [lot](/docs/inventory/lot-numbers) remembers the
  quantity it started with, and every change is recorded as a **transaction**.
  On-hand is recomputed from that ledger whenever it's needed. This gives a
  complete, auditable history of every movement.
</Callout>

## The six quantities

These are the figures shown on the **Inventory** tab of the
[item details page](/docs/inventory/item-details#inventory).

| Quantity | Meaning | How it's calculated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **On Hand** | Physical quantity currently in stock across all lots of the item. | For each lot, its starting quantity plus or minus every transaction; summed over all of the item's lots. |
| **On Order** | Quantity from open purchase orders that has not yet been received. | The sum of purchase order line quantities for the item, excluding lines on orders already fully received. |
| **Allocated** | Quantity committed to confirmed batches that have not yet consumed their materials. | The sum of bill-of-material quantities on batch records in a confirmed state (queued, staging materials, compounding, awaiting materials, or completed). |
| **Soft Allocated** | Quantity committed to **draft** batches not yet confirmed. Becomes Allocated once the batch is confirmed. | The sum of bill-of-material quantities on batch records still in draft. |
| **Available** | The quantity free to be allocated right now. | On Hand − Allocated |
| **Soft Availability** | The quantity that would remain free if every current draft batch were confirmed. | On Hand − Allocated − Soft Allocated |

### Why two kinds of allocation

A batch starts life as a **draft** — a plan that might still change or be
cancelled. Material claimed by a draft is **soft allocated**: it warns you the
stock is spoken for tentatively, but it doesn't yet hold the stock back from
"real" availability. Once the batch is **confirmed**, its material becomes
**allocated** (hard), and `Available` drops accordingly. Tracking both lets you
see your firm commitments (`Available`) and your planned commitments
(`Soft Availability`) side by side.

## How stock changes

Every movement of stock is a transaction against a lot. Each transaction type
either **adds to** or **deducts from** on-hand:

| Transaction | Effect on On Hand |
| --- | --- |
| Procurement (a lot received from a purchase order) | Increase |
| Adjustment — addition (manual correction or gain) | Increase |
| Batch consumption (material used in production) | Decrease |
| Adjustment — removal (manual correction or loss) | Decrease |
| Order fulfillment removal (stock shipped out) | Decrease |

Because on-hand is the running total of a lot's starting quantity plus all of
these transactions, it can always be reconstructed and explained.

## A worked example

Suppose an item is counted in kilograms and has:

- Two lots on the shelf totaling **100 kg** → **On Hand = 100**
- An open purchase order line for **50 kg** not yet received → **On Order = 50**
- A confirmed batch needing **30 kg** → **Allocated = 30**
- A draft batch tentatively needing **20 kg** → **Soft Allocated = 20**

From those:

- **Available** = 100 − 30 = **70 kg** — free to commit right now.
- **Soft Availability** = 100 − 30 − 20 = **50 kg** — what would be left if the
  draft batch were confirmed.

The 50 kg on order doesn't change availability until it's received, at which
point it becomes a new lot and raises On Hand.
