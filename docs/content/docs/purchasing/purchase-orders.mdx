A **purchase order** (PO) is a commitment to buy specific items, in specific
quantities, from one supplier. It is the document you send to the supplier and the
record receiving uses to book goods into stock. Every PO belongs to a single
supplier and carries its own line items, notes, accounting detail, and activity
history.

The purchase orders board lives at `/purchasing/purchase-orders`.

## The purchase orders list

The board is a table of every active order, newest first. Archived orders are
hidden. Each row summarizes one PO:

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **PO #** | The order's reference number, assigned automatically in sequence. |
| **Supplier** | The supplier the order is placed with. |
| **Status** | Where the order sits in its pipeline, shown as a colored tag. |
| **Accounting** | The state of the order's accounting detail (payment and paperwork), shown as a colored tag. Blank until accounting is started. |
| **Total ($)** | The sum of every line item's quantity times its price. |
| **Updated** | When the order last changed. |

You can filter the table by **Supplier**, **Status**, and **Accounting**. Clicking
a row opens that order.

## Creating a purchase order

There are two ways to start a new order, and both open it as a **Draft**:

- **From the purchase orders board:** press **New Purchase Order** (or the `ctrl+c`
  shortcut), then pick a supplier. Typing filters the supplier list, and pressing
  Enter selects the first match. The order opens empty, ready for you to add items.
- **From a procurement request:** on a request's detail page, use the **Linked
  POs** card to add a new order. This creates the PO for the request's supplier and
  seeds it with the requested item as a first line, then links the order back to
  the request so both stay in sync.

## The purchase order tabs

An open order is organized into tabs:

- **Items:** the line items being ordered. This is where most of the work happens.
- **Accounting:** the order's payment and paperwork detail, with its own Details,
  Notes, and Files sub-tabs. See the [Accounting](/docs/accounting) module for how
  this fits into reconciliation.
- **Notes:** internal, public, and supplier notes for the order (covered below).
- **Activity:** a log of every change to the order, filterable by action and user.
- **Options:** high-level actions. **Duplicate** clones the order and all of its
  items into a new Draft, and **Archive** removes the order from the board after a
  confirmation.

## Line items

Line items are managed on the **Items** tab. By default the tab is read-only. Press
**Modify** (or `ctrl+e`) to edit, then **Add Item** (or `ctrl+r`) to search for an
item by name, alias, or reference code and add it. A newly added line starts at
quantity 0 and price 0, using the item's inventory unit as its unit of measurement.

Each line exposes:

- **Quantity:** how much to order.
- **Price Per Unit:** the agreed price per unit of measurement.
- **UOM:** the unit the supplier sells in, chosen from any unit in the system.
- **Total:** quantity times price, calculated for you.

### Units and conversions

A purchase order records quantities in the unit the supplier sells in, which is not
always the unit you stock the item in. The PO itself does no conversion: it stores
exactly what you ordered in the chosen purchase UOM.

The conversion to the item's inventory unit happens later, at
[receiving](/docs/receiving). When goods are booked in, Lumexia converts the
received quantity into the item's inventory UOM using either a standard conversion
or a supplier-specific discrete conversion. For how those conversions are defined,
see [Unit of Measurement Conversions](/docs/inventory/uom-conversions). If a
supplier sells in a non-standard pack unit, make sure a discrete conversion exists
for that item and supplier, or receiving will not be able to convert it.

## Notes

An order supports three kinds of notes, and the difference is about who sees them:

| Note type | Where it appears |
| --- | --- |
| **Internal** | Internal use only. These never appear on any purchase order PDF. |
| **Public** | Print on the PDF for this specific order. |
| **Supplier** | Stored on the supplier, and print on every order placed with that supplier. |

Supplier notes are convenient for standing instructions that apply to a vendor
across all orders, while public notes are specific to a single order.

## Supplier aliases on purchase orders

Suppliers often refer to an item by their own name or catalogue code rather than
yours. When a [supplier alias](/docs/inventory/items#aliases) exists for the order's
supplier and item, the line shows the supplier's name for the item, and a tooltip
lists every alias plus your true item name. The generated PO prints the supplier's
alias, so the document reads in terms the supplier recognizes.

Supplier aliases are created on the item, as described in the inventory
[aliases](/docs/inventory/items#aliases) documentation. A supplier's linked aliases
are also listed on the supplier's [Aliases tab](/docs/purchasing/suppliers).

## Generating the purchase order document

The order header has a **PO** button that generates and downloads the purchase
order as a PDF. The document includes your company details and logo, the shipping
and supplier addresses, the line items with quantities and prices, the public and
supplier notes, and the order totals.

### Company details

The company information on the PDF comes from **Settings → Company**
(`/settings/company`). Configure it there once and every generated PO uses it. The
fields are grouped into:

- **Company:** name, phone, and email.
- **Address:** the street, city, state, and zip that appear as the shipping
  address.
- **Purchasing Contact:** the contact name and email shown to the supplier.

The company logo is uploaded on the same settings page and is drawn on the PDF.

## Pushing a purchase order through the pipeline

A PO advances through a series of stages. Use the **Next** and **Previous** buttons
in the header to move it along; each change is applied to the order and to all of
its line items.

| Stage | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | The order is being built and has not been sent. |
| **Pending** | The order has been sent and is awaiting the supplier's response. |
| **Confirmed / Awaiting Delivery** | The supplier confirmed and the goods are on the way. |
| **Received** | Everything on the order has been booked into stock. |
| **Partially Received** | Some, but not all, of the order has arrived. |

Manual advancement only goes as far as **Confirmed / Awaiting Delivery**. The last
two stages are set by receiving rather than by hand.

<Callout>
  Receiving can only see an order once it reaches **Confirmed / Awaiting Delivery**.
  The header's **Receiving** button warns you if the order is not yet at that stage.
  When the shipment is booked in, receiving moves the order to Received or Partially
  Received automatically. See the [Receiving](/docs/receiving) module for the rest
  of that workflow.
</Callout>

## Purchasing insights

Lumexia rolls up purchase orders into spend and frequency figures in two places:

- **On the supplier:** the supplier's [Purchases tab](/docs/purchasing/suppliers)
  shows order count, total spent, and average order value over a date range, plus a
  spend-over-time chart. Its Items tab breaks the same history down per item, with
  total spent, last price, the units purchased in, and how often the item was
  bought.
- **On the item:** the item details **Purchasing** tab shows the same story from the
  item's side, comparing suppliers and pricing over time. See the
  [item details](/docs/inventory/item-details) page.

Together these answer how much you spend with a supplier, how much you spend on an
item, and how frequently you buy it.
